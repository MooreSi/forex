## Added after 03:30

Everything above was written at 03:30. What followed:

**[043](../todo/bugs/043-coming-back-to-an-idle-page-reloads-it.md) — your
reload symptom, diagnosed and fixed.** You said the page reloads when you come
back after a while. Two keepalive layers disagreed and the tighter one won
silently: uvicorn's `ws_ping_timeout` was deliberately set to 60 s, but NiceGUI
derives engine.io's own deadline from `reconnect_timeout`, which gave it
**12 s**. A backgrounded tab, throttled by the browser, misses that and the
session is dropped. `reconnect_timeout` is now 150, making both layers 60 s.
**Please confirm whether it still reloads** — it depends on real browser
behaviour I cannot reproduce.

**The reload is also stall cause 3.** Rebuilding the page fires ~25 panel
refreshes in one tick: 2,065 ms, measured at 06:36 when you opened it. Between
03:30 and 06:35, with nobody on the page, there were **no stalls at all**. The
rebuild itself is not fixed — that is a frontend change across a dozen render
functions.

**[020](../todo/reversal-engine/020-losses-exceed-the-stop.md) can now measure
the right thing.** `sl_dist` is the stop at OPEN, and the EA **trails without
logging a thing** — traced live: a stop moved from 4438.26 to 4431.23 with no
line in either log. So a trade that clears TP1 and is stopped out is stopped at
a far tighter stop, and `mae_pts / sl_dist` reads under 1.0 with nothing wrong.
A new `last_seen_sl` column now records the stop actually in force. I had said
this was not worth building; that was wrong, and it is collecting now.

**The trend gate can only act on 70% of signals.** `htf_bias_at_fill` is
neutral 14% of the time and neutral does not block, by design — while this
file's own history says neutral loses about as much as counter-bias does
(184 trades, -$1,234). "The trend gate is on" reads like full coverage and is
not.

**Verified, not changed:** the EA honours every template TP level and lot
fraction exactly (checked against 24 hours of partial closes), open positions'
stops match the broker exactly, the ledger matches the broker to the penny, and
no table that should be written is silently failing.

**Three false alarms I caught before reporting them:** a Telegram SELL that
looked like a missed gate (the bias was neutral, not opposed), the limit-order
path that looked ungated (it is schedule-gated at fill, by design), and a
reachability audit that flagged working code (my grep could not see functions
passed as references).
